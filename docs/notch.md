# Yoru in the notch

Added 2026-11-13, off by default (Settings → On the Mac → "Yoru in the notch"). A small Yoru lives around the Mac's camera notch: closed, he's a black shape on either side of it, a little Yoru on the left and what he's doing on the right (the microphone's level while he listens, a dot while he thinks or speaks, the cards waiting for an answer, what's unread, or how many coding agents are working). With the pointer on him, or while he hears or talks, the same black shape grows downwards from the notch, so it reads as part of the hardware: pure black with no grey cards in it, flush with the top of the screen, its top corners curving out into the menu bar, nothing drawn under the camera. In it, as plain text, the last exchange (what you said dimmed, his answer in white; with Gemini Live, your words appear as you say them, the newest at the end) or the card to allow or deny, the coding agents at work (up to three: Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode conversations written to in the last minutes, yours or Yoru's, with a button to stop his), then a field to type in and round buttons to talk (click the microphone to turn it on, click it again when you're done), stop him and open the app. Hold **⌥ Space** anywhere to talk to him through the Mac's microphone; he answers through the Mac's speakers. The first time the notch comes up, macOS asks whether Yoru may use the microphone; if the answer was no, a crossed-out microphone sits on his right and the open panel says so, with a button to System Settings (macOS doesn't ask twice). On a screen without a notch he sits in the middle of the menu bar.

## A board made of software

The notch is a board as far as the rest of the host knows, so the whole voice runs as it does on the real one: the transcription, the brain, the voice, Gemini Live, the follow-ups and Smart Turn, the confirmations, the reactions.

- **`core/src/notch.rs`** answers the board's protocol (protocol v2, `docs/protocol.md` in the firmware's repository) over a socket pair inside the process: `hello` (firmware `notch`, id `mac`, no capabilities, so no cloud keys are sent and nothing offers to flash it), `tools/list` (`react` as the real board names it, `get_state`, `set_volume`), the cards' tools answered and shown by the app instead, `voice/state`, `voice/listen`, `voice/stop`, and the speech on `audio_down`, reported back with `playback_end`. What it hears goes up as `utterance_start` with `trigger: "button"` (push-to-talk) or `"follow_up"`, `audio_up` frames and `utterance_end`.
- **`Link::open("mac")`** (`link.rs`) starts it; the manager (`bridge/manager.rs`) runs its session beside the boards while it's on (`notch::set_enabled`), outside `max_boards`, and doesn't count it when saying no board is found. A real board comes first for timers and alerts. Its session doesn't attach to the hub (Claude Code drives the real board) and doesn't overwrite the board's cached tool list.
- **Hearing.** Push-to-talk sends what was heard while the key was held. After an answer, the follow-up the host asks for listens without a key: a simple level detector (about −42 dBFS for three frames in a row) tells speech from silence, the pause the host gives ends it, and nothing heard in `wait_ms` closes it. No wake word.
- **The Mac's audio.** `yoru-audio talk` (`audio/Sources/yoru-audio/Talk.swift`): one AVAudioEngine with Apple's voice processing on, so the microphone doesn't hear Yoru from the speakers and you can talk over him; other sounds aren't ducked. The microphone runs only while it's needed, and the engine stops after 20 seconds unused, taking the Mac's microphone indicator with it. Started cold, voice processing takes about a second, which ate the first words of a press, so the engine starts as soon as the pointer comes onto the notch (if the permission is already given), is checked every 5 seconds while the pointer stays there (it may have come before the notch's session was up), and a click finds it on; the shortcut key still starts it cold. The notch then also sends `about_to_talk`, and with Gemini Live on the host opens its session ahead (kept three minutes for the next conversation), so the first press doesn't wait about a second for it. JSON lines on stdin and stdout, 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM in base64.

## The window

- **`app/src-tauri/src/notch.rs`**: a transparent, borderless window 620 × 520 points at the top of the screen with the notch (else the main one), put above the menu bar, on every Space and over full-screen apps through AppKit (`NSStatusWindowLevel + 1`). The notch's width comes from `NSScreen`'s `auxiliaryTopLeftArea` and `auxiliaryTopRightArea`, its height from `safeAreaInsets`. Clicks go through it everywhere but on what the page draws: the page reports that rectangle (`notch_content`), and a thread follows the pointer, turning `set_ignore_cursor_events` off on it and telling the page (`notch-pointer`). Plugging a screen in or out places it again.
- **The microphone's permission** is the app's (`NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` in `Info.plist`; `yoru-audio` runs as part of it). `yoru-audio microphone [--ask]` says where it stands (`granted`, `denied`, `restricted`, `unknown`) and with `--ask` brings up macOS's prompt if the user never answered; the app calls it through `notch_microphone`, and `open_microphone_privacy` opens System Settings at Privacy & Security, Microphone. The panel asks on start, looks again when it opens and after an error.
- **⌥ Space** is a global shortcut (`tauri-plugin-global-shortcut`), registered only while the notch is on; if another app holds it, the panel's button still works.
- **`app/src/Notch.svelte`**: the same bundle as the main window, mounted in the notch's window (`window.__YORU_NOTCH__`, or `?notch` in the browser preview). It keeps the store (conversation, cards, inbox) and listens to the `notch` event for the voice's state, the microphone's level, reactions and errands.
- The notch isn't in the app's board switcher, firmware updates or Flash.

## Privacy

The Mac's microphone is used only while you hold the key or the button is on, or in the few seconds of a follow-up after an answer. What it hears goes where the board's voice would go: the transcription you chose (this computer or your provider) or Gemini Live.
