# Yoru in the notch

Added 2026-11-13, off by default (Settings → On the Mac → "Yoru in the notch"). A small Yoru lives around the Mac's camera notch: closed, he's a black shape on either side of it, a little Yoru on the left and what he's doing on the right (the microphone's level while he listens, a dot while he thinks or speaks, the cards waiting for an answer, what's unread, or how many coding agents are working). With the pointer on him, or while he hears or talks, the same black shape grows downwards from the notch, so it reads as part of the hardware: pure black with no grey cards in it, flush with the top of the screen, its top corners curving out into the menu bar, nothing drawn under the camera. In it, as plain text, the last exchange (what you said dimmed, then the tools his answer used as small pills, a word for each and what it asked in grey: *Ricerca web · ultime notizie oggi*, *Gmail · search threads*, a green dot or a red one when it failed, three at most, the tool search's own housekeeping left out; then his answer in white; with Gemini Live, your words appear as you say them, the newest at the end) or the card to allow or deny (the notch opens on its own while a card waits: a tool to allow, or a meeting to record, docs/meetings.md, Detection), the coding agents at work only with the pointer on their count (up to three: Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode conversations written to in the last minutes, yours or Yoru's, with a button to stop his; they stay until the pointer leaves the notch), then a field to type in and round buttons to talk (click the microphone to turn it on, click it again when you're done), stop him and open the app. Hold **⌥ Space** anywhere to talk to him through the Mac's microphone; he answers through the Mac's speakers. The app's first run asks for the microphone (it's required, see app.md); if it was turned off later, a crossed-out microphone sits on his right and the open panel says so, with a button to System Settings (macOS doesn't ask twice). On a screen without a notch he sits in the middle of the menu bar.

## A board made of software

The notch is a board as far as the rest of the host knows, so the whole voice runs as it does on the real one: the transcription, the brain, the voice, Gemini Live, the follow-ups and Smart Turn, the confirmations, the reactions.

- **`core/src/notch.rs`** answers the board's protocol (protocol v2, `docs/protocol.md` in the firmware's repository) over a socket pair inside the process: `hello` (firmware `notch`, id `mac`, no capabilities, so no cloud keys are sent and nothing offers to flash it), `tools/list` (`react` as the real board names it, `get_state`, `set_volume`), the cards' tools answered and shown by the app instead, `voice/state`, `voice/listen`, `voice/stop`, and the speech on `audio_down`, reported back with `playback_end`. What it hears goes up as `utterance_start` with `trigger: "button"` (push-to-talk) or `"follow_up"`, `audio_up` frames and `utterance_end`.
- **`Link::open("mac")`** (`link.rs`) starts it; the manager (`bridge/manager.rs`) runs its session beside the boards while it's on (`notch::set_enabled`), outside `max_boards`, and doesn't count it when saying no board is found. A real board comes first for timers and alerts. Its session doesn't attach to the hub (Claude Code drives the real board) and doesn't overwrite the board's cached tool list.
- **Hearing.** Push-to-talk sends what was heard while the key was held (60 seconds at most, as the board's `max_length`), and pressing it silences a timer that's ringing, as the board's wake word does. After an answer, the follow-up the host asks for listens without a key: a simple level detector (about −42 dBFS for three frames in a row) tells speech from silence, and nothing heard in `wait_ms` closes it. As on the board, half a second of quiet still counts as speech (the VAD's hangover); then `utterance_pause` goes to the host, and the pause the host gives ends the utterance, or the shorter or longer one `voice/pause` sets: Smart Turn holds it at 1.5 s and stops the utterance as soon as the sentence sounds finished. No wake word.
- **The same answers.** The notch answers `voice/state` with a `voice_state` notification, as the board does, so the host's idea of its state (alerts wait for idle) follows. `react` is refused while a conversation is on, as on the board, and the mirror plays every reaction the board has (low or narrowed eyes for the ones without a gesture). The brain is told the request came through the notch (`via: the notch` in the context line, from `Host::in_notch`): its prompts describe both bodies, so it doesn't promise a screen, a wake word or a Tasks app there. Its three tools never replace the board's on the tools' cache on disk.
- **Typed in its field,** a request uses the notch's tools (`notch::typed_tools`, `notch::call_typed`), not the board's on the desk, and is marked `via: the notch` too.
- **Timers and alerts** ring and are said on a real board whenever one is connected, even a timer set from the notch or right after a conversation in it (`alerts::choose`); the notch says them only when it's alone.
- **The Mac's audio.** `yoru-audio talk` (`audio/Sources/yoru-audio/Talk.swift`): one AVAudioEngine with Apple's voice processing on, so the microphone doesn't hear Yoru from the speakers and you can talk over him; other sounds aren't ducked. It starts only when you talk (the key or the button), never on opening the notch: holding the microphone puts Bluetooth headphones in their call mode (16 kHz mono, muffled). Voice processing takes about a second to start, so until the microphone hears the notch says *Getting ready…* with a grey spinner (a `starting` state shown only in the notch: the host hears of the utterance once the microphone is on, and a follow-up's wait counts from then); a key let go meanwhile ends it without telling the host. While it runs, music or video another app plays is paused (`Media.swift`: MediaRemote's pause, sent only when CoreAudio says another app is playing) and started again when the helper ends. The helper ends, letting go of the audio devices, as soon as the notch closes while Yoru isn't hearing or talking, or after 20 seconds unused with the notch open; its stdin closed, it restarts the music it paused and exits (killed after 2 seconds otherwise). Stopping only its engine wasn't enough: it left Bluetooth headphones in their call mode until the helper exited. With the pointer on the notch it sends `about_to_talk` (every 5 seconds while the pointer stays), and with Gemini Live on the host opens its session ahead (kept three minutes for the next conversation), so the first press doesn't wait about a second for it. JSON lines on stdin and stdout, 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM in base64.

## The window

- **`app/src-tauri/src/notch.rs`**: a transparent, borderless window 620 × 520 points at the top of the screen with the notch (else the main one), put above the menu bar, on every Space and over full-screen apps through AppKit (`NSStatusWindowLevel + 1`). The notch's width comes from `NSScreen`'s `auxiliaryTopLeftArea` and `auxiliaryTopRightArea`, its height from `safeAreaInsets`. Clicks go through it everywhere but on what the page draws: the page reports that rectangle (`notch_content`), and a thread follows the pointer, turning `set_ignore_cursor_events` off on it and telling the page (`notch-pointer`). Plugging a screen in or out places it again.
- **The microphone's permission** is the app's (`NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` in `Info.plist`; `yoru-audio` runs as part of it), read and asked by the app's own process (`app/src-tauri/src/permissions.rs`: `permissions`, `request_permission`, `open_privacy`). Until 2026-11-18 `yoru-audio microphone --ask` asked for it, and macOS showed no prompt for the helper. The panel looks again when it opens and after an error; it no longer asks on start, the first run does.
- **⌥ Space** is a global shortcut (`tauri-plugin-global-shortcut`), registered only while the notch is on; if another app holds it, the panel's button still works.
- **`app/src/Notch.svelte`**: the same bundle as the main window, mounted in the notch's window (`window.__YORU_NOTCH__`, or `?notch` in the browser preview). It keeps the store (conversation, cards, inbox) and listens to the `notch` event for the voice's state, the microphone's level, reactions and errands.
- The notch isn't in the app's board switcher, firmware updates or Flash.

## Privacy

The Mac's microphone is used only while you hold the key or the button is on, or in the few seconds of a follow-up after an answer. What it hears goes where the board's voice would go: the transcription you chose (this computer or your provider) or Gemini Live.
